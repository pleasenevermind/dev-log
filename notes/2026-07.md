# 2026-07

## 2026-07-14

- Back after a break. Read the ReAct paper properly: interleaving reasoning traces and actions helps when the environment gives useful observations, and hurts when it does not.

## 2026-07-16

- Reflexion: self-reflection improves retry performance, but only with an external signal. Without one, the model just rewrites the same mistake with more confidence.
- Toolformer: self-supervised tool labeling is clever, but the filter is the important part. A model that labels its own successes will amplify its own errors.
- Common thread: these methods assume a verifier. Agent papers that skip the verifier are hard to reproduce.

## 2026-07-17

- Self-RAG: retrieval on demand, with reflection tokens. The idea is good; the implementation complexity is high for a small harness.
- GraphRAG paper: community summaries help global questions, not local lookup. Matches what I saw in February: narrow lift, real indexing cost.

## 2026-07-20

- DPO: preference optimization without a reward model. The subtle part is the reference model and the beta that controls how far the policy can drift.

## 2026-07-21

- LoRA: rank and alpha matter more than people admit. r=8 on a small instruction set was enough; r=64 overfit in fewer steps.
- QLoRA: 4-bit base plus adapters makes a 7B fine-tune fit on a single 24GB card. Throughput is slow but usable for a small set.

## 2026-07-23

- Chinchilla scaling: compute-optimal is roughly 20 tokens per parameter. Most released models are trained well past that because inference cost dominates.

## 2026-07-24

- FlashAttention: the gain is memory traffic, not FLOPs. Explains why long-context training got cheaper without a change in the math.
- Mamba and SSMs: linear scaling in sequence length is attractive, but the recall benchmarks still favor attention. Hybrid stacks make sense.
